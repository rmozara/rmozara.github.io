Editorial Notes

All translations in this collection are intentionally literal. They reproduce the grammatical meaning of the original text without adding interpretive ideas not explicitly present in the Sanskrit or Gurmukhi sources.

Artificial intelligence tools (ChatGPT) were used to assist with grammar clarification, literal phrasing, and transliteration, under human review and correction. Editorial choices, formatting, and final interpretations remain the responsibility of the compiler.

Brief historical notes (e.g. period, textual origin) are provided when known and remain strictly descriptive.

Translations and notes are prepared with careful attention to literal accuracy, but they may contain unintentional errors or omissions. This collection is not intended as an authoritative scholarly edition; it is a learning resource compiled in good faith.

Vedic Mantras

Gāyatrī Mantra

ॐ भूर्भुवः स्वः
तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यं
भर्गो देवस्य धीमहि
धियो यो नः प्रचोदयात्

Oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ

tat savitur vareṇyaṃ

bhargo devasya dhīmahi

dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt

Literal translation:
Oṃ — invoking earth, atmosphere and heaven.
That radiance of Savitṛ, worthy of reverence,
the divine effulgence on which we meditate,
may it stimulate our intellects.

Notes
  • bhūr — “earth”
  • bhuvaḥ — “atmosphere / life-realm”
  • svaḥ — “heaven / luminous realm”
  • The three terms form an invocation and do not constitute a sentence.
  • Used as a prefatory recitation line before the Gāyatrī in later ritual traditions.
  • Savitṛ — solar deity, source of wisdom/illumination
  • bhargaḥ — purifying radiance (not “light” literally)
  • dhīmahi — “we meditate” (1st person plural, optative nuance)
  • pracodayāt — optative “may it impel / guide”
  • Verse from Ṛgveda 3.62.10, traditionally attributed to the sage Viśvāmitra.

Post-Vedic / Puranic

Lokāḥ Samastāḥ Sukhino Bhavantu

लोकाः समस्ताः सुखिनो भवन्तु

Lokāḥ samastāḥ sukhino bhavantu

Literal translation:
May all worlds be happy.

Notes
  • Lokāḥ — “worlds”
  • samastāḥ — “all”
  • sukhino — “in happiness”
  • bhavantu — optative “may they be”
  • Modern liturgical sentence; not found in the Vedas, Upaniṣads, or Purāṇas.
  • In widespread use since the 19th century in South Indian temple traditions (especially within Śaṅkara lineages).

Śrī Aurobindo’s Gāyatrī

ॐ तत् सवितुर् वरं रूपं
ज्योतिः परस्य धीमहि
यो नः सत्यान दीपयेत्

Oṃ tat savitur varam rūpaṃ

jyotir parasya dhīmahi

yo naḥ satyena dīpayet

Literal translation:
Oṃ — that supreme form of Savitṛ;
we meditate on the radiance of the Supreme;
may it illumine us through truth.

Notes
  • Savitṛ — impelling solar power (Vedic deity, not the physical sun)
  • jyotis — “radiance / spiritual light”
  • dīpayet — optative “may it illuminate”
  • Post-Vedic liturgical adaptation associated with the Integral Yoga tradition.

Oṁ klīṁ ātmane namaḥ

ॐ क्लीं आत्मने नमः

oṃ klīṃ ātmane namaḥ

Literal translation:
Salutations to the Self.

Notes
  • klīṃ — bīja (seed sound), non-lexical; ritually linked to attraction/devotional śakti.
  • ātmane — “to the Self” (dative singular of ātman).
  • namaḥ — “salutations / reverence.”
  • Translation reflects only lexical meaning; interpretive associations of the bīja are noted separately.
  • Occurs in later Tantric devotional practice, not in Vedic or early classical sources.

Stotra / Nāmāvalī (Hymns of Names)

स्तोत्र–नामावली

Dvādaśa–Nāmāni (Twelve Names of Sūrya)

सूर्यस्य द्वादशनामानि

IAST: sūryasya dvādaśa–nāmāni

Literal: The Twelve Names of Sūrya.

ॐ मित्राय नमः
Oṃ mitrāya namaḥ — Salutations to Mitra (the benevolent one)

ॐ रवये नमः
Oṃ ravaye namaḥ — Salutations to Ravi (the shining one)

ॐ सूर्याय नमः
Oṃ sūryāya namaḥ — Salutations to Sūrya (the sun)

ॐ भानवे नमः
Oṃ bhānave namaḥ — Salutations to Bhānu (the radiant one)

ॐ खगाय नमः
Oṃ khagāya namaḥ — Salutations to Khaga (the sky-mover)

ॐ पुष्णे नमः
Oṃ puṣṇe namaḥ — Salutations to Puṣan (the nourisher)

ॐ हिरण्यगर्भाय नमः
Oṃ hiraṇyagarbhāya namaḥ — Salutations to Hiraṇyagarbha (the golden seed)

ॐ मरीचये नमः
Oṃ marīcaye namaḥ — Salutations to Marīci (the ray)

ॐ आदित्याय नमः
Oṃ ādityāya namaḥ — Salutations to Āditya (son of Aditi)

ॐ सवित्रे नमः
Oṃ savitre namaḥ — Salutations to Savitṛ (the impelling force)

ॐ अर्काय नमः
Oṃ arkāya namaḥ — Salutations to Arka (heat / radiance)

ॐ भास्कराय नमः
Oṃ bhāskarāya namaḥ — Salutations to Bhāskara (the light-maker)

Notes
  • namaḥ + dative form means “reverential salutations to X.”
  • Names denote functional aspects of solar luminosity, not separate deities.
  • Traditional liturgical recitation; regional variations exist.

Shabad (Gurmukhi / Sikh Tradition)

ਸ਼ਬਦ (ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ / ਸਿੱਖ ਪਰੰਪਰਾ)

Re Man Shabad (Rāga Rāmkalī)

ਰੇ ਮਨ ਸ਼ਬਦ (ਰਾਗ ਰਾਮਕਲੀ)

IAST: re man śabad (rāga rāmkalī)

Literal: The “Re Man” hymn in Rāga Rāmkalī.

ਰੇ ਮਨ ਏਹ ਬਿਧਿ ਜੋਗੁ ਕਮਾਓ ॥
re man ehi bidh yog kamāo — O mind, practice union by this method.

ਸੀੰਘੀ ਸਾਚਿ ਇਕਪਤ ਕੰਠਾਲਾ ॥
sīṅghī sāci ekapaṭ kanthālā — Fasten truth firmly as your single necklace.

ਧਿਆਨਿ ਭੀਭੂਤਿ ਚੜ੍ਹਾਓ ॥
dhyāni bhībūt chaṛhāo — Apply meditation like sacred ash.

ਤਾਤੀ ਗਹੈ ਆਤਮ ਬਸੁ ਕਰਿ ॥
tātī gahai ātam basu kari — Grasp the heat; establish control of the self.

ਬਿਛਾਓ ਨਾਮ ਅਧਾਰੰਗ ॥
bichhāo nām adhāraṅg — Spread the Name as your foundation.

ਬਾਜੇ ਪਰਮ ਤਾਰ ਤਤੁ ਹਰਿ ਕਾ ॥
bāje param tār tatu har kā — The supreme string of the Divine essence resounds.

ਉਪਜੈ ਰਾਗ ਰਸਾਰੰਗ ॥
upajai rāg rasāraṅg — A melody of essence arises.

ਉਘਟੈ ਤਾਨ ਤਰੰਗ ਰੰਗ ਅਤੈ ਗਿਆਨੁ ਗੀਤੁ ਬੰਦਾਨੰਗ ॥
ughaṭai tān taraṅg raṅg atai giānu gīt bandānang — Tones, waves, and colors unfold, and knowledge is bound in song.

ਚਕਿ ਚਕਿ ਰਹਹਿ ਦੇਵ ਦਾਨਵ ਮੂਨਿ ॥
chak chak rahahi dev dānav mūni — Gods, demons, and sages remain absorbed.

ਛਕਿ ਛਕਿ ਬਿਓਮ ਬਿਵਾਨੰਗ ॥
chhak chhak biom bivānang — Immersed repeatedly in the vast expanse.

ਆਤਮ ਉਪਦੇਸੁ ਭਗਤਿ ਸੰਜਮੁ ॥
ātam upades bhagat sanjam — Self-instruction is devotion and discipline.

ਜਾਪੁ ਸੋ ਅਜਪਾ ਜਾਪੈ ॥
jāp so ajapā jāpai — The unspoken mantra is naturally recited.

ਸਦਾ ਰਹੈ ਕੰਜਨ ਸੀ ਕਾਇਆ ॥
sadā rahai kanjan sī kāyā — The body remains as pure as gold.

ਕਾਲੁ ਨ ਕਬਹੂ ਬਿਆਪੈ ॥
kāl na kabahū biyāpai — Death never approaches.

Notes
  • 16th-century Sikh hymn attributed to Guru Nānak (SGGS 921, Rāga Rāmkalī).
  • Literal translation is grammatical, not interpretive.
  • The vocative re man means “O mind”, addressing oneself directly.
  • Imagery refers to inner discipline, not external yogic technique.
  • Chanting Version (phonetic): Ray man eh bidh jog kamao
    Singhee saach akapat kanthala
    Dhiaan bibhoot charhaao
    Taatee gaho aatam bas kar kee
    Bichaa naam adhaarang

    Ughatay taan tarang rang atay giaan geet bandhaanang
